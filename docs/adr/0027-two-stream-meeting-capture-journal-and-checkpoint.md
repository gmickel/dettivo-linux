# 0027. A meeting is two takes on one clock, a journal and a live checkpoint; the daemon's next start promotes what a killed one left

Status: Accepted 2026-09-04

## What this gives you

A meeting records your microphone and the other side as two aligned tracks, keeps recording when your headset disappears, and survives a crash or a power loss: the next daemon start turns what was captured into a partial meeting you keep or discard, with every take intact. Transcription, diarization and notes build on files whose shape is fixed here.

## Situation

The Windows port's meeting lane lost meetings to device loss and to crashes, because capture, recovery and transcription were built together and the recovery had nothing stable to read. The macOS port keeps a `LiveMeetingCheckpoint` (schema version 1: session id, the live segments, cursors, `isFinalizing`, `lastError`) and promotes stale sessions to partial with `isPartial`, `chunksCompleted` and `chunksTotal`. ADR 0006 chose PipeWire so two streams can share a clock and a sink monitor can be captured; ADR 0014 left meeting tables to this spec. The contract (`docs/api/dettivo-ipc-v1.md` section 8.3) fixes the `meetings.*` shapes and reserves `meetings.delete`; the Windows port adds `acknowledge_meeting_disclosure`. Three questions were open: how the two tracks stay aligned and what a device loss does to them, what a killed daemon leaves behind and how the next start reads it, and which of the contract's reserved verbs this port implements.

## Decision

`crates/dettivo-meeting` owns the meeting session; the daemon drives it through `meetings.*`.

- **Two takes on one clock.** The microphone (`[audio] input_device` or the default source) and the system track (`[audio] system_source`: the default sink's monitor through `stream.capture.sink`, or a pinned sink) open as two PipeWire captures at 16 kHz mono; both take writers count offsets from the meeting's one `Instant`, so `takes.json` and `system-takes.json` carry `start_offset_ms` and `start_offset_ns` on one base. A microphone loss (the default moved, the pinned node left the graph, the stream ended) closes the take, reopens on the current default, and starts `microphone-<n>.wav` with `gap_before = true`; a loss that finds no source keeps the system track alone. The system stream follows the default sink inside PipeWire and never opens a new take. Without a sink the meeting is room audio and the journal says so.
- **The journal** (`journal.jsonl`, one JSON line per event with the offset on the meeting clock) is the record the merger and a person read: `start`, `system_unavailable`, `checkpoint`, `device_switch`, `device_lost`, `gap`, `system_switch`, `system_ended`, `error`, `stop`, `cancel`, `recovered`.
- **The live checkpoint** (`live-checkpoint.json`, schema version 1) is written atomically every `[meetings] checkpoint_interval_seconds` (15) beside a flush of both takes: the meeting id and start, every take with its offset and flushed samples, the retained segment tail (`segments`, `next_sequence`, empty until transcription), `chunks_completed`, `chunks_total`, `is_finalizing`, `last_error`. A clean stop or cancel removes it. `system.capabilities.meetings.checkpoint_schema` says which schema the daemon writes.
- **Recovery at start.** Before the socket listens, every meeting row still `recording` or `stopping` is promoted to `partial`: WAV headers are repaired from the file length (a killed `hound` writer never finalised them), the duration is the longer track, the checkpoint supplies the chunk counts, and a checkpoint that cannot be read leaves `0/0` with `recovery_reason` naming why. `meetings.status` lists every partial meeting under `recoverable`; `meetings.recover` keeps it as `stopped` with its audio, `meetings.discard` removes the row and the directory. The audio is never removed by the recovery itself.
- **The store.** Migration `0004-meetings` adds the `meetings` table with the macOS `MeetingSession` fields (title and its source, status, start and end, duration, language, provider and model, `is_partial`, the chunk counts, the disclosure time, the audio facts, texts and segments, errors) and an FTS5 index; `transcripts.list` walks one timeline over both tables with a `created_at|kind|seq` cursor, a dictation before a meeting started at the same instant.
- **The gates and the verbs.** `meetings.start` refuses in order: `sessionActive`, `meetingDisclosureRequired` (the acknowledgement lives once in `state.toml` with its time; `meetings.disclosure.get` returns the macOS notice, `meetings.disclosure.acknowledge` records it), `engineWithoutTimestamps` (a provider ADR 0018 keeps dictation-only, with the settings route in the details). Stop answers `stopping` at once and the worker reports `stopped`; a stop on a settled meeting answers its state. `meetings.delete` is implemented with the contract's `artifact_policy` and `retention.delete` is true; the contract's `delete.error-not-implemented` fixture, which describes a daemon with `retention.delete = false`, is retired. `HistoryStatus` and `meeting.state` gain `stopping`, `stopped` and `partial`, and the payload the Linux fields `previous_state`, `reason`, `duration_ms`, `microphone_takes`, `system_audio`.
- **Imports.** `transcripts.import { target_kind: "meeting" }` runs the ADR 0022 pipeline into a meeting row (source `audioImport`) after the disclosure gate and answers with `is_partial = false` and the running job, as the contract states.

## Consequences

- The next spec's merger reads two WAV tracks with offsets in nanoseconds on one clock plus a journal of gaps; it does not need to reconstruct alignment from stream timestamps. The bound is PipeWire's own scheduling of two streams on one graph; on the virtual rig the first takes start within a few milliseconds of each other and the scenario fails above 200 ms.
- A pinned device that vanishes mid-meeting is detected the moment its node leaves the graph (a watcher on registry removals), not from the stream state, because PipeWire keeps a stream on a vanished target alive and feeds it silence; `meeting_rig` proves it by unloading the microphone's null sink and finding `microphone-2.wav` with the gap marker.
- A meeting whose checkpoint interval is 15 s can lose at most 15 s of sidecar counts, never audio: the takes are on disk as they are written and the header repair counts every byte.
- `stop` is asynchronous by design so the `stopping` state is visible; a client that needs the settled row polls `meetings.status` or reads `meeting.state`. `transcribing` and `completed` sit between `stopping` and the final state once the transcription spec lands.
- The macOS disclosure text is reproduced with `transcribed` where the macOS source reads `dettivod`, a rename artifact in that repository.
- The QA seed carries the contract's sample meeting so the `meetings.*` and meeting-kind `transcripts.*` fixtures replay; the fixtures that mint ids or need an active or partial meeting are stateful and covered by name in `crates/dettivod/tests/meetings.rs` and `meetings_import.rs`.
