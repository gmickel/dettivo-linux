# 0039. The meetings pack measures throughput through the daemon's own finalisation path and proves the GPU tier did its work on the GPU

Status: Accepted 2026-09-05; amended by [0051](0051-the-release-gate-passes-on-validated-evidence-bound-to-the-tested-binaries.md): `--record` writes the block into the latest report in place, never a copy under a new date

## What this gives you

One command, `dettivo-qa pack meetings`, proves the whole meeting lane on the machine in front of you and writes the numbers the strategy promises for long audio: two-source capture on the virtual rig with a device swap, a kill and recovery, the live transcript against its golden, diarization against the turns golden, the five export formats, the three meetings screens on both drivers, a two-voice fixture whose every word must survive capture, transcription and the speaker pass, and then meeting throughput for NFR-4 and NFR-5 measured the way a user experiences it, through `transcripts.import` and the finalisation pipeline rather than an engine's CLI mode. The same command with `--cpu` records the CPU tier with `base.en`, `--record` files both rows into the checked-in benchmark report, and a GPU run carries the proof that the engine process was on the GPU while it worked. It is the phase 4 exit check and the third named step of the release gate.

## Situation

Phase 4 ends with capture (ADR 0027), live transcription (ADR 0031), diarization (ADR 0035), notes and analysis (ADR 0036) and the meetings screens (ADR 0038) each proven by its own scenario, and the masterplan's exit criteria ask for them together on the rig and in CI, with the NFR-4 and NFR-5 figures measured and checked in per release. The benchmark suite (ADR 0029) measures transcription throughput through the engines' CLI modes, which is inference alone: it does not time the chunker, the overlap merger, the filler filter, the polish pass or the store, and its diarization row has been a placeholder since the suite landed. The dictation pack (ADR 0017) is the shape a release step takes, one ordered list of steps into one report with the blockers named, and the GUI packs (ADR 0037) added the per-route scans. Three things were open: where the meeting throughput number comes from, how a GPU-tier figure proves it was the GPU's rather than a CPU fallback the daemon reported as a lower tier after the fact, and how a fixture proves that specific words survive the whole lane rather than a word error rate under a ceiling.

## Decision

The pack is `crates/dettivo-qa/src/pack/meetings.rs`, fifteen steps in order: `meeting_rig`, `meeting_recovery`, `meeting_live`, `diarization_der` (the ADR 0035 pipeline under 0.20 with two speakers), `meeting_export_goldens` (`cargo test -p dettivo-storage --test meeting_export`, a command step), `meetings_seeded`, `meetings_live_gui` and `meetings_import_gui` on `atspi` then `cua`, `meeting_token_coverage`, `meeting_throughput`, `diarization_throughput` and `gpu_workload_proof`. Every step must pass except the `cua` drives without the tool, the GPU proof, and `meeting_rig` on a machine without PipeWire, where the mock steps carry the capture path and the skip is a blocker the report names. The scenarios are unchanged; `--cpu` reaches them as `DETTIVO_FORCE_CPU=1` through the profile's environment.

The token fixture is two Piper voices rendered once and checked in (`crates/dettivo-qa/fixtures/meetings/alpha.mic.wav`, `alpha.system.wav`, `scripts/qa/make-alpha-fixture.py`): Alice on the microphone track, Ben answering on the system track once she has finished, and `alpha.tokens.json` naming the words each source must carry (`alice`, `launch`, `checklist` on the microphone; `ben`, `pricing`, `decision`, `ship`, `friday` on the system). The scenario plays them through the rig's null sinks where PipeWire runs and through `DETTIVO_MOCK_MIC` and `DETTIVO_MOCK_SYSTEM_AUDIO` elsewhere, stops, runs the speaker pass over the system track with the count pinned to one, names the two speakers, pulls the `md` export, and fails naming the token and its source when a word is missing from that source's segments, naming the count when the row does not carry two speakers, and naming the export when Alice and Ben are not both before a line.

Throughput is measured through the product: `meeting_throughput` builds five minutes of meeting audio (the alpha microphone track once, then the jfk clip repeated with a second of silence after each copy, mixed onto one clock), uploads it through the transfer verbs and imports it with `transcripts.import { target_kind: "meeting" }` on a daemon in its own profile with diarization and analysis off, so the chunked pipeline, the merger, the filler filter, the polish pass and the store are all inside the clock. The realtime factor is the audio length over the wall time from the job's first `decoding` progress event to `done`; the engine, model, backend and reason come from `speech.engines`, the tier and its reason from `system.capabilities.platform`, and the target from `crates/dettivo-qa/src/nfr.rs` by that tier (NFR-4 on the GPU, NFR-5 on the CPU). `diarization_throughput` runs `dettivo-engine-diarize` in CLI mode over the same system track and holds its factor to NFR-4's four times realtime. The harness measures its own CPU share with the daemon idle before the import and again during it; a share over ten percent is recorded as a warning that the load was not the product's alone, never hidden. The model is `--model`, else `base.en` under `--cpu` (the NFR-5 model) and `large-v3-turbo` on the GPU tier when on disk, else `tiny.en`; an id the catalogue does not know exits 2 naming the Whisper ids it does, and a model not on disk fails the step naming `dettivo speech download`. Under `--cpu` an engine that still reports the Vulkan backend fails the step by name.

The GPU proof is a sampler that runs for the whole import: every 500 ms it reads the daemon's `dettivo-engine-*` children from `/proc` and the GPU's process table, `nvidia-smi` on NVIDIA (the whole table, because a Vulkan compute process is listed as a graphics client, which the compute-apps query alone would miss) or `gpu_busy_percent` under `/sys/class/drm` on AMD. `gpu_workload_proof` judges the samples afterwards: a pass names the engine pid and the samples it appeared in, a skip names the counter the machine lacks or the CPU tier, and a GPU tier whose engine never appeared fails by name.

The report is `meetings-pack.json` and `meetings-pack.md` in the ADR 0017 shape with `measurements.meetings` (`meeting_rtf`, `meeting_rtf_nfr`, `meeting_rtf_target`, `meeting_rtf_met`, `diarization_rtf`, `diarization_rtf_target`, `diarization_rtf_met`, `tier`, `engine`, `model`, `backend`, `audio_ms`, `wall_ms`, `fixture_sha`, `harness_cpu_pct`, `harness_warning`, `gpu_workload_proof`, `token_coverage`, `forced_cpu`). `--record` files the same figures as the `meetings` block of the checked-in benchmark report for the host and tier (`docs/reports/benchmarks/<date>-<host>-<tier>.json`, today's file or the latest for that host and tier carried forward under today's date with the block naming the pack's own commit) and re-renders the README through the benchmark writer, so the release notes quote one table. The tier in the file name is `cpu` under `--cpu` and the daemon's tier otherwise, so a desktop's two rows never merge. Under CI the figures are recorded and never gated, as the dictation pack does.

## Consequences

The first two rows are in `docs/reports/benchmarks/2026-09-05-thor-gpu.json` and `2026-09-05-thor-cpu.json` (the same desktop as ADR 0029, an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X3D with an RTX 4090, the Whisper engine built on Vulkan into `target/vulkan`, a debug cargo profile with the native inference code built Release), rendered in the README beside them, which the release gate quotes.

| Figure | GPU tier (`large-v3-turbo`, Vulkan) | CPU tier (`--cpu`, `base.en`) | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meeting throughput through `transcripts.import` (311.7 s of audio) | 62.05x realtime (5.0 s) | 21.87x realtime (14.3 s) | NFR-4 >= 20x, NFR-5 >= 5x: both met |
| Diarization throughput (300 s system track, `dettivo-engine-diarize` on the CPU) | 13.62x realtime | 14.59x realtime | NFR-4 >= 4x: met |
| GPU workload proof | engine pid listed by `nvidia-smi` in 10 of 10 samples | skipped: no GPU work expected | - |
| Token coverage | 8 of 8 on their source, rig capture | 8 of 8, rig capture | every token |

The GPU figure is a third of the benchmark suite's CLI-mode figure for the same engine (89x, ADR 0029) because the finalisation path is inside the clock; NFR-4's twenty times realtime holds with headroom either way. The CPU row is `--cpu` on the same desktop until the CPU-only VM exists, and the pack's own environment name (host and tier) keeps that row from merging with the VM's when it arrives.

The throughput figure is lower than the benchmark suite's for the same engine and model because it includes everything after inference; that is the point, and the two are recorded side by side under one host and tier rather than reconciled. The diarization engine runs on the CPU on every tier (ADR 0035), so its factor is a CPU figure under the GPU row too and is held to NFR-4's four times realtime on both.

Four things the first runs turned up are recorded for their owners. The three meetings screens pass on the fallback driver in every run; on cua-driver `meetings_seeded` never sees the renamed speaker after typing into the popover and `meetings_import_gui` cannot click the dialog's Import button, which cua-driver lists only in its markdown rendering and not as an actionable element (the driver now refuses that by name instead of sending a malformed token), and `meetings_live_gui` on either driver occasionally reports no provisional segment before the first final one or a `meetings.status` call that times out while a CPU chunk decodes; those belong to the meetings GUI spec (ADR 0038) and the driver, and until they are fixed the pack's run of record on this desktop is `--continue` with the cua rows failed and named, and its `--record` block says the pack failed on another step. The speaker pass and an import's end rewrote the meeting's `metadata.json` with the row alone, dropping the take lists the finalisation had written (ADR 0027); the rig capture step caught it and the rewrite now keeps the rest of the file (`dettivo_meeting::update_metadata_row`). The rig step needs PipeWire, so a container skips it by name and the token scenario's mock path is the capture the CI run proves. The `md` export lists both sides at the same timestamp when both takes start on the meeting clock together; the export goldens still hold and the ordering question stays with the export's owner.

Bounds: the pack runs under Xvfb in CI with `tiny.en` and the mock fixtures, so its figures there are the mock tier's and gate nothing; the harness CPU share is the runner process alone, not the display server or the compositor, and a busy machine shows in the load average the figures carry; the token fixture proves eight words on two voices, not a word error rate, and a second fixture with overlapping speech belongs to the soak spec; the CPU-only VM's row arrives when that machine does, through the same `just qa-pack-meetings-cpu`.

The comparison/proof rules above are amended by [ADR 0054](0054-delete-duplicate-work-and-keep-one-owner.md). Approved renders now use full-resolution pixel colour comparison; allocation and aggregate GPU activity no longer establish inference attribution. Historical measurements and their original outcomes remain evidence of their stated method.
