# 0103. Enhanced rejects a rewrite of implausible length and stops a local generation at an output budget

Status: Accepted 2026-11-08

## What this gives you

An Enhanced rewrite that balloons past twice your words, collapses to a fraction of them, or quietly drops a `not` never reaches your text: the deterministic Polish text goes in instead. A local generation that would run on stops at 1.5 times the dictation's tokens plus 32, so a runaway costs a fraction of a second instead of the 1024 tokens `[engines.llm] max_tokens` allows. On the private eval sets the protected, required and forbidden metrics did not move.

## Situation

Three simple rules catch whole classes of model failure: reject an output over 2x the input's word count, or under 0.4x once the input passes 8 words; stop generation at 1.5x the input tokens plus 32; reject a short deletion of 2 words or fewer that removes only meaningful words. The output guard (`crates/dettivo-language/src/enhanced/guard.rs`, ported from the macOS `PolishOutputGuard`) already had length checks of its own, so each rule was compared with what it did:

| Rule | What the guard had | The gap, with an input that slipped through |
|---|---|---|
| Over 2x the words | a character cap, `max(chars + 40, chars x 1.8)`, only for a dictation of 24 characters or more | `ship it tomorrow` (3 words, 16 characters) rewritten as `Ship it tomorrow, and tell everyone you know.` (8 words) |
| Under 0.4x past 8 words | a contraction rule for non-technical input that rejects 3 or more dropped words only when the rewrite does not end a sentence | a 15-word dictation about moving a launch review rewritten as `Move the review.` |
| Output budget | none: every Enhanced request asked for `[engines.llm] max_tokens`, 1024 by default, and the provider's finish reason was never read | a generation that never ends its turn ran until 1024 tokens or `[llm] timeout_ms` |
| Short deletion of meaningful words | a check for questions, repair cues and repeated lists that needs 2 or more dropped words of 3 or more letters outside its stop-word list, and `not` is a stop word | `we should not ship the release on Friday` rewritten as `We should ship the release on Friday.` |

The Qwen3 tokenizer, run over the 431 transcripts of the private eval sets, puts a median of 0.22 tokens on a character and at most 0.49, and spends 14 tokens on each output marker the prompt requires.

## Decision

All four gaps close, in `guard/length.rs` beside the guard.

**Length.** A rewrite with more than twice the dictation's words fails with the guard's expansion reason. One with under 0.4 of them, when the dictation has more than eight, fails with its contraction reason, so the Enhanced pass inserts the deterministic text without a repair pass when that text passes the guard. A rewrite that carries a path, a file name, a flag or a code span is exempt from the second rule: a spoken path (`dettivo slash services slash ... dot swift`, 13 words) rightly becomes one token, and the first eval run rejected exactly that row until the exemption went in. The exemption looks at the rewrite, because the guard's technical check on the dictation also matches the English words `let` and `class`. The length rules run after the refusal and input-request checks, so a long refusal keeps its more specific reason. A dictation in a script written without spaces between words (Chinese, Japanese, Thai) has no word count to compare, so only the character cap applies to it.

**Short deletion.** A rewrite that is the deterministic draft with one or two words taken out, the words it keeps in the draft's order and nothing else changed but casing and punctuation, fails with `output dropped the dictated word <words>`. The draft goes in straight away and without a notice, even when it equals the dictation, because the draft is the text the deletion was measured against and a repair pass would only spend time getting back to it. The draft is the reference instead of the dictation because the deterministic transforms drop words of their own (`R and D` becomes `R&D`). The rewrite's words are aligned with the draft's as written, in order, before any word is excused, and each removed word is then judged where it stood in the draft. Hesitations, hedges and intensifiers (`um`, `really`, `probably`) may go anywhere. `you know`, `I mean`, `kind of` and `sort of` may go only as whole phrases, and `so`, `well`, `like`, `okay`, `yeah`, `right` and `anyway` only where they open a sentence or a paragraph, because elsewhere they carry meaning (`turn right`, `I like it`). A copy of a word said several times in a row may go while the rewrite keeps another copy of that run, and a run never crosses a sentence boundary, so `never never` losing both copies is a deletion and so is `I said no. No one should merge it.` losing its second `no`. Because the alignment comes first, neither a filler between two words, nor a repeat the rewrite keeps, nor a deletion that forms a new phrase (`you do not know` to `you know`) hides a dropped `not`. The deletion is judged before the guard's other checks, so a rewrite that drops a dictated number along with it also takes the draft at once. A word repeated elsewhere in the sentence never excuses another word dropped beside it, and a reordered sentence is a rewrite. A deletion of three or more words is outside the rule.

**Output budget.** `RewriteRequest` carries `max_tokens`, and the Enhanced pass sets it to 1.5 times the dictation's tokens plus 32, plus 32 for the output markers. The tokens are estimated at one per three characters, which the tokenizer stays under on 421 of the 431 transcripts, so the estimate errs long and never cuts a sound rewrite short. The daemon's local engine generates at most that many tokens and never more than `[engines.llm] max_tokens`. When the engine reports `finish_reason = length`, the pass falls back at once with `guard_rejected` and `the rewrite ran past its output budget`, without a repair pass, since a repair would run to the same budget again. Ollama and an OpenAI-compatible endpoint keep their own limits; neither reports a finish reason the pass reads.

## Consequences

`dettivo-qa polish-eval` measured the change on an RTX 4090 over Vulkan with `qwen3-4b-instruct-2507` (the default) and `qwen3-1.7b`, on the two private held-out sets (10 rows each) and on the 275 rows of the private corpus v3 (`--split all`). The baseline ran twice to measure run-to-run spread. Medians on the same code moved by up to 14 ms between those two runs.

Exact match rose on both corpus runs, from 18.2% to 20.4% on the 4B and from 29.8% to 30.2% on the 1.7B. Seventeen rows changed outcome, all of them through the short-deletion rule, and in every one the gold keeps the words the model dropped. The inserted draft matches the gold exactly in 10 of the 17. The rule caught the 1.7B dropping German `nicht` from `Bitte ändere SettingsView.swift nicht` in two rows, which turned "do not change" into "change". In the fifteen 4B rows it caught a dropped `please`, `everything`, `that`, `those`, `as well`, `blah`, `some`, `But`, `the`, `Roadmap:`, `like, yeah` and a mid-sentence `right`. The guard rejection and fallback rates did not move, because these rows take the draft without a notice.

A first version of the rule, reviewed on gpt-6.1-sol, had three faults the review caught. Any removed word that survived elsewhere excused the whole deletion, so `We should not ship the the release.` could lose its `not`. It ignored word order. And a rejected rewrite whose draft equalled the dictation ran two repair passes before the draft went in, which raised the 4B corpus median to 383 ms. The PR's review bots then found twelve more over six passes: ambiguous words such as `right` and `you` counted as fillers everywhere, a deletion that also dropped a number missed the fast path, the collapse exemption fired on any dictation containing the word `let`, Chinese text counted as a single word, a repeated run such as `never never` or `the the the the` excused or counted copy by copy or across a sentence boundary, and five cases where filtering fillers and collapsing repeats before aligning the words hid a dropped `not` or misread a paragraph opener. The last five share one cause, so the matcher now aligns the words first and excuses them afterwards. The quality figures above are from the final rule. The medians are from the run before those last twelve fixes. The final run's medians were taken while another process loaded the GPU, so on unchanged rows it measured 38 to 102 ms slower than the run before it on identical code paths. Those fixes only send more rows to the draft without a second generation, and they add no generation anywhere.

The output budget never fired on the 570 rows. No eval row runs away, and the 9 to 14 s p95 of the held-out sets is the first row that reaches the model, which pays for the engine's load. Every median is at or below both baseline runs. On rows whose outcome did not change, the paired per-row median moved between -18.5 and -3 ms against the second baseline run, inside the 14 ms spread. The 4B corpus median fell further, to 344 ms, because its twelve changed rows now insert the draft without a second generation.

The tests prove each rule where it applies. `guard::length` tests the four rejections, the inputs each existing rule let through, a `not` dropped beside a repeated word, and the cases that must pass (fillers, a repeat in a row, a rewrite that adds a word, a reordered sentence, a three-word cut, a spoken path). `enhanced::tests::a_runaway_generation_stops_at_the_budget_and_falls_back` asserts the budget a request carries and the fallback after one attempt. `enhanced::tests::a_rewrite_that_drops_a_meaningful_word_inserts_the_draft` and `a_dropped_word_inserts_the_draft_at_once_when_it_equals_the_dictation` run a dropped `not` through the whole pass in one attempt. `dettivo-engine-llm`'s `a_generation_that_would_run_on_stops_at_max_tokens` holds the engine to the budget with the real Qwen3 1.7B model.

Bounds: a deletion of three or more meaningful words passes this rule. Since a repeated run counts in full when none of it stays, one corpus row whose rewrite dropped `like` and a whole `blah blah blah` now counts four words and keeps the rewrite, where an earlier version of the rule put in the draft, which matches the gold. The matcher aligns words greedily, so `I think, you know, you should ship` cleaned of `you know` reads as dropping `know` and the second `you`, and the draft goes in instead of that rewrite. The budget is an estimate, and on the 10 of 431 transcripts where the tokenizer exceeds one token per three characters it is that much tighter than 1.5 times the real tokens. The filler list is English with the German hesitation sounds. A dropped meaningful word in another language counts, but a dropped filler of another language is rejected as meaningful.
