# 0047. Delivery guards the window itself, checks it again before typing, and never types a take twice

Status: Accepted 2026-09-06

## What this gives you

Releasing the key puts the text in the window the key went down over, or on the clipboard with the reason, and never anywhere else: not in a second window of the same application, not in a window that took the focus while a portal dialog or a long take was in flight, and not twice because a helper died after typing half of it. A compositor binding runs in the same dictation mode as a daemon-side hotkey, the first-run screen and the meeting transcript say what happened rather than what was hoped, and the QA rig fails when its evidence is incomplete instead of repairing it.

## Situation

Ten gpt-6-astra reviews of main 28b4b7d (`docs/reports/reviews/2026-09-06-astra/`) grouped twelve findings under delivery and the target window (`.flow/specs/fn-45-cleanup-delivery-and-the-target-window.md`). Each was verified against the code and a test before anything changed. The ones that held:

- The session target was an app id and a pid (ADR 0016), so two windows of one process both passed the guards, and a probe that failed or saw nothing at the key press became an absent target, which the chain read as "no guard" and typed into whatever had the focus when the take was transcribed.
- The guards were checked once, before the backends probed their availability and before libei's portal handshake, which can take seconds and can itself move the focus. The eventual typing used the earlier decision.
- A backend error was one string, and the chain handed the whole text to the next backend on any error. `xdotool` with a non-ASCII text runs at 40 ms a key under a 20 s command timeout: a 500-character take timed out after typing a prefix and the next backend typed all of it again. A clipboard restore that failed after a paste did the same.
- `Target.secure_field` was always `false`: no probe read the focused control, so the clipboard-only branch behind it never ran while the contract promised the protection.
- `dettivo dictation start` and `toggle` sent `mode = raw` when the binding named none, so the compositor path overrode the configured `[dictation] mode` that the portal and evdev paths honoured.
- A meeting's take offsets stamped the writer's creation, after both sources had opened, while the live lanes both started at zero: the two clocks disagreed and neither described the samples.
- The Hyprland hotkey scenario installed fixed chords the user might own and ignored a cleanup that failed; the first-run drive repaired a conflicting insertion before judging it; the first-insert benchmark reported a figure from one surviving warm run out of ten.
- The window could shrink to 600 px while History's sidebar and list alone need 600; `r` renamed a speaker the transcript rows had not selected; the Try it step read "Inserted" for every result and promised the clipboard for every reason.

## Decision

- **The guard is the window.** The probe keeps the compositor's identity of the focused window (the Hyprland address, the X11 window id) beside the app id and the pid; it never leaves the daemon. A dictation session records it (`SessionTarget.window`) and the chain compares it before any backend runs (`guards::check_guards`), so another window of the same process is `CONFLICT`. A probe that saw nothing at the key press is an unverified origin (`SessionTarget::unverified`), distinct from an unguarded request such as `reinsert_last`: the take is inserted in clipboard-only mode and the result is `copied_to_clipboard` with reason `origin_unverified`. This amends ADR 0016's target from a process to a window.
- **The origin is checked again right before the first key.** Every backend receives the origin check in its context (`Ctx::recheck`) and runs it after its own setup and before its first key: after the Wayland connection and the keymap upload, after the portal handshake and the EI device resume, before the helper command starts, before the paste keystroke. The virtual keyboard runs it before every batch too. A focus that moved is `failed` with reason `target_changed: ...` and nothing is typed; a move between batches ends the typing there and is reported as a partial delivery. Insertions run one at a time under the service's lock.
- **A failure says whether text reached the target.** A backend fails as `Before` (nothing delivered; the next backend may take the text), `During` (delivery had begun or cannot be ruled out: a helper that exited with an error or ran out of time, a compositor that dropped the connection mid-text; the insertion ends as `failed` with reason `partial_delivery: <backend>: ...` and the text is never typed again) or `FocusMoved`. A clipboard restore that fails after a paste is a warning on a successful insertion. This amends ADR 0007's rule that a backend which fails mid-way hands over to the next.
- **No claim without a probe.** `Target.secure_field` leaves the `insert.target` result, its fixture and the delta register. Secure-field detection returns with a probe that detects it, and not before.
- **An omitted mode is the configured mode.** `dictation.start.mode` is optional on Linux (`docs/api/linux-deltas.md`): absent means `[dictation] mode`, the default the portal and evdev hotkeys already applied; the CLI sends no mode it was not given.
- **One clock from the instant a source opened.** The meeting machine stamps each source when it opens; the first take counts from that instant (`TakeWriter::start_take_at`) and the live windower of that lane starts at the same origin, so a system track that opens 300 ms after the microphone lands 300 ms later in the live view and in the finalisation alike. This sharpens ADR 0031's "one clock" to a stated origin.
- **The rig owns what it removes and judges what it saw.** The Hyprland scenario reserves four Ctrl+Alt+Shift function keys that `hyprctl binds -j` shows free, unbinds them afterwards and asks the compositor to confirm; a chord left bound fails the scenario. The first-run drive requires the take's own insertion to land and asserts the post-Done refusal (`failed`, `target_is_self`, no backend). The first-insert bench qualifies a row only when every warm run completed; a short row keeps its failures and partial spread and fails the step with the count.
- **The screens say what happened.** The window's minimum width is `Theme.appWindowMinWidth`, the sidebar plus the widest fixed column plus a detail at least a rail wide, so every route lays out at the minimum. The transcript hands its focused row's speaker to the detail through one signal, so `r`, the click and the talk-time bar share one selection. The Try it step derives its label, explanation and clipboard claim from the take's outcome: `inserted`, `copied_to_clipboard` or `failed`.

## Consequences

- A window that closes during a take, a second window of the same app, or a focus that lands on Dettivo itself all end as `CONFLICT` or `target_changed` with the transcript kept; `dettivo dictation reinsert-last` hands it over again without a guard, as before. An origin the probe could not name costs a paste: the text is on the clipboard with the reason.
- A helper that dies mid-text leaves a prefix in the window and a `partial_delivery` result rather than a doubled text; the OSD and History show the reason, and the user pastes the rest from History. Whether a prefix landed is not known for `xdotool` and `ydotool`; the daemon assumes it did.
- Each delivery costs one more probe round trip (one Hyprland IPC query or one X11 property read, well under a millisecond) and the virtual keyboard one per batch of 160 characters.
- `insert.target` no longer carries `secure_field`; a client that read it sees the field gone, which the delta register records.
- A client that sent `mode` keeps working; one that omitted it under the copied contract was `INVALID_PARAMS` and now starts in the configured mode.
- The meeting clock's origin is the instant the daemon's open call returned, not the first sample the stream delivered; the two differ by the stream's own start-up, the same for both tracks in practice and not measured here.
- The Hyprland scenario skips with the reason on a compositor with fewer than four free chords. The bench fails on a flaky machine instead of producing a headline figure from the runs that survived.
- Superseded lines: ADR 0016's target as app id and pid, ADR 0007's hand-over on any mid-way failure. Both records keep their other decisions.
