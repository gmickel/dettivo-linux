# 0031. A meeting is transcribed twice by one merger: live in windows while it records, and in full from every take when it stops

Status: Accepted 2026-09-05

## What this gives you

Words appear while the meeting runs and a complete transcript is on the row when it stops. Both sides of the call arrive as segments on one clock, marked `you` or `remote`, provisional first and final once the engine will not revise them; the transcript that lands after the stop is built from every take that was recorded, never from the live view, and a meeting a crash interrupted is finalised the same way. What you hear through your own microphone from the speakers is not written twice.

## Situation

ADR 0027 fixed what a meeting leaves on disk: two takes on one clock, a journal and a live checkpoint. ADR 0022 built the offline chunked pipeline with its timestamp merger for imports. The macOS product streams live segments from windows of each stream (`LiveMeetingTranscriptionConfig`: a 3000 ms window, 450 ms overlap, a 900 ms tick, a 1200 ms boundary merge gap, 800 ms of cross-source padding, the speech activation and continuation floors) and finalises from the preserved audio (FR-A2); its `LiveMeetingSegmentMerger.shouldSuppress` decides which side keeps a phrase both tracks heard. Three questions were open: how the live path shares the merger with the offline one so the two transcripts agree, what a slow engine does to the live view, and which side wins when both tracks carry the same words.

## Decision

`crates/dettivo-transcribe` gains the live path; `crates/dettivo-meeting` drives it beside the capture and runs the finalisation; the daemon publishes the results.

- **Windows at the macOS tuning.** `live::Windower` keeps each source's samples on the meeting clock and cuts a window every `live_tick_ms` (900) that reaches back `live_overlap_ms` (450) into the previous one and never exceeds `live_window_ms` (3000). A window under `speech_floor_rms` (0.0065, the activation floor; 70 % of it while the previous window was speech, the continuation floor) is silence and never reaches the engine. An engine more than two ticks behind has the oldest audio skipped, and the skip is a `live_skip` line in the journal and a gap on the next segment; finalisation covers the skipped span anyway.
- **One merger for both paths.** `live::Merger` feeds a source's windows through the ADR 0022 chunk merger as consecutive chunks, so overlapping windows reconcile by timestamp exactly as import chunks do. Whatever ends more than `boundary_merge_gap_ms` (1200) before the newest audio is final and numbered (`you-12`); the rest is the provisional tail (`you-p1`) that the next window replaces whole. A device switch hardens the old take's tail and puts the gap on the first segment of the new one.
- **The live thread.** One thread per meeting receives both tracks' samples from the capture worker, cuts what is due on each lane, and sends one window at a time through the engine, the remote lane first when both are due. Every update is a `meeting.segment` event (`meeting_id`, `source`, `segment_id`, `provisional`, `start_ms`, `end_ms`, `text`, `words`, `gap_before_ms`), and the final segments plus the provisional tail are the checkpoint's segment tail and the `live_segment_count` and `live_last_end_ms` on `meeting.state`. The engine is frozen at start: a model not on disk is refused there naming the download command, and a provider that answers a window without timestamps ends the live path with a `live_unavailable` journal line while the capture continues.
- **Finalisation from every take.** The stop closes the takes, then every preserved take of both tracks goes through the offline pipeline (chunker, engine, merger, hallucination filter, near-silence guard), each take's segments are shifted onto the meeting clock by its offset, a take that began after a gap carries the gap on its first segment, the journal's take-less gaps become gap markers, and the two sources interleave. `meetings.stop` answers the contract's `transcribing` job at once; `job.progress` counts chunks over every take; the segments and the text land on the row, the state goes `stopping`, `stopped`, `transcribing`, `completed`. `meetings.recover` runs the same finalisation on a partial meeting's takes. A cancel between chunks leaves the meeting `stopped` with its audio; a failed finalisation leaves it `failed` with the reason and the audio.
- **The cross-source rule.** Segments sort by start on the meeting clock, the remote side first on a tie. A microphone segment that is a filler (`yeah`, `okay`, `thank you`, or four characters or less) and overlaps, within `cross_source_padding_ms` (800), a remote segment at least twice as long and eighteen characters or more is the speakers heard through the microphone and is dropped; every other microphone segment keeps its words. This is the macOS rule as written, and it is the only suppression: two people talking over each other both stay in the transcript.
- **Configuration and surfaces.** The tuning is `[meetings] live`, `live_window_ms`, `live_overlap_ms`, `live_tick_ms`, `boundary_merge_gap_ms`, `cross_source_padding_ms` and `speech_floor_rms`; `live = false` runs the finalisation alone. `dettivo meetings segments <id> [--follow]` shows the transcript by side and span and streams a running meeting first. The pill shows Listening with the meeting's elapsed time while it records.

## Consequences

- The live transcript and the final one come from the same merger and the same filters, so they differ only where the finalisation had more context: whole takes instead of three-second windows. The live view is for reading along; the row is the record.
- The live path costs one engine call per tick per source that carries speech. On tiny.en on a CPU that is well under a tick; a larger model that falls behind loses live coverage, journaled as `live_skip`, and never loses transcript coverage. Nothing is invented for silence: a gated window makes no call, and whisper.cpp's `[BLANK_AUDIO]` and its siblings are on the hallucination list.
- The engine's own segment boundaries decide where a segment starts once leading silence sits inside a take: whisper.cpp folds the silence into the first segment, so the interleave orders by those starts, not by the first word. The `meeting_live` drive judges the transcript by words per source and by order of the starts, and holds the first side as evidence.
- `meeting_live` proves the path on the mock tracks in CI with tiny.en: the jfk clip as the other side and its last phrase as the microphone, provisional then final per source, the transcribing stop, the progress per chunk, and the transcript against the two-source golden by word error rate. `meeting_rig` and `meeting_recovery` follow the stop and the recover into the finalisation and require it to complete over every take; the session tests cover the device switch with its gap marker, the lagging engine, the cancel and the provider without timestamps.
- Speaker labels beyond `you` and `remote`, notes and the meeting screens are later specs; a meeting re-run stays `NOT_IMPLEMENTED`.
