# 0029. The NFR targets are calibrated per hardware tier from a checked-in benchmark report, and the daemon names the tier

Status: Accepted 2026-09-05, amended 2026-09-06 by 0049 (the tier leaves the CPU-only diarization engine out)

## What this gives you

Every number Dettivo for Linux promises is one you can reproduce: `just bench` measures time to first insert, transcription throughput, the language model, the daemon's idle footprint and startup from the fixtures the tests already use, files one report per machine and tier under `docs/reports/benchmarks/` with each figure beside its target, and `dettivo doctor` tells you which tier your machine is and why. The targets the reports are held to live in one file and were set from the first two reports on the development machine, so a release note that says "met" or "not met" means the same thing on every machine.

## Situation

The masterplan stated the NFRs per hardware tier with initial figures: NFR-1 first insert p50 at or under 1.0 s on the GPU tier, NFR-2 at or under 2.5 s on the CPU tier, NFR-4 meeting throughput at or over ten times realtime on the GPU (diarization four times), NFR-5 at or over twice realtime on the CPU, NFR-6 a daemon idle resident set at or under 60 MB with the engines unloaded after their timeout, and NFR-8 the socket ready within 300 ms with the app's first frame under 300 ms. Those figures were estimates, marked to be calibrated by this spec, and until now nothing measured them together: the dictation pack (ADR 0017) reported NFR-1 with a GPU target and left the CPU tier without one, the parakeet alignment and the LLM idle-unload reports each measured one thing, and no report said which tier a machine was on.

Three things had to be decided. What "tier" means and who says it: the pack inferred it from the Whisper engine's backend after the fact, which a client or a benchmark cannot ask for in advance. Where the benchmark numbers come from: a drive through a window measures the real chain but needs a display and the accessibility bus, which the CI runner and half the development desktops lack, while an engine's CLI mode measures inference alone. And what to do when a measurement misses an estimate: keep the estimate as an aspiration, or move the target to what the shipped engines do on the hardware named and say so.

## Decision

The daemon names the tier. `system.capabilities.platform.tier` is `gpu` when a Vulkan device is installed and no engine that has loaded fell back to the CPU, and `cpu` otherwise or under `DETTIVO_FORCE_CPU=1`, which the supervisor now honours for every engine it spawns; `platform.tier_reason` says which of those it was (`dettivo-speech::tier`). Every `speech.engines` row carries `memory_bytes`, the process's resident set, beside the language model's device memory in `llm.engine.status`. `dettivo doctor` is the FR-D5 snapshot: compositor, session type, tier, the desktop portals found on the bus, the platform block, insertion backends, hotkeys, the snippet, audio devices, engines with backend, reason, state and memory, models per provider, the language model's provider order and readiness, the socket and units, history, the pill, REST and MCP, and the `DETTIVO_*` overrides, in one shape a golden pins by structure the way the contract replay levels machine-specific results.

`dettivo-qa bench` is the suite (`crates/dettivo-qa/src/bench/`). `first_insert` reuses the dictation pack's timing scenario without a window: a daemon in a bench profile dictates the 11 s fixture through the mock microphone once cold and ten times warm per speech engine, measuring the stop request to the completion event; the insert leg goes through the mock inserter, so the figure is the engine tier's and the pack's `first_insert_timing` stays the measurement of the real chain. `stt_throughput` repeats the clip into five minutes and runs each speech engine's CLI mode over it, the realtime factor with the model load included. `llm_rewrite` sends the Polish goldens' inputs through `dettivo-engine-llm --prompt` and records latency and tokens per second past a load-only run. `idle_footprint` loads both engines, watches `speech.engines` until they are gone and names one that stays, then samples the daemon's resident set over five idle minutes. `startup` times ten daemon starts to the first ping and takes the app's first frame from the `app_routes` drive when a display exists. `diarization` is a placeholder until its spec lands. Each step records every sample with p50, p95, min and max, the model, the backend and the engine's reason; the report carries the host, the GPU as `vulkaninfo` enumerates it, the fixtures' hashes, the models' catalogue checksums and the commit. `--cpu` forces the CPU tier, `--quick` runs three iterations for CI, `--engines` points at the Vulkan build, and `--out` files the report as `<date>-<host>-<tier>.json` and rewrites the README table from the latest report per host and tier. A missing model skips its row naming the download; a missed target is recorded and never gates.

The README index selects the same `<date>-<host>-cpu.json` and `<date>-<host>-gpu.json` filenames as the release gate. Diarization accuracy reports can share the directory without being parsed as benchmark suites. A selected suite file that fails typed parsing still stops index generation and names the file.

The targets live in `crates/dettivo-qa/src/nfr.rs`, each with its initial and its calibrated figure, and the dictation pack's NFR lines read them from there: the CPU tier is held to NFR-2 now rather than recorded without a target. The calibrated figures are the ones below, set from the first GPU and CPU reports on the development machine with headroom for a slower machine of the same tier, and a target moves only in a new decision record.

## Consequences

The first two reports are `docs/reports/benchmarks/2026-09-05-thor-gpu.json` and `2026-09-05-thor-cpu.json`: an AMD Ryzen 9 7950X3D with an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 on the proprietary driver (610.57.04), 64 GB, with the engines built on Vulkan, the product's default models (`whisper/large-v3-turbo`, `parakeet/parakeet-v3`, `llm/qwen3-4b-instruct-2507`), the daemon and engines from a debug cargo profile (the native inference code is built Release either way). The CPU report is `--cpu` on the same desktop; the CPU-only VM the masterplan named does not exist yet, and the report says so in its notes. Both reports carry the machine's load average at the start and the end of the run, because the development machine is shared with other builds and a busy run must say so beside its numbers.

| Target | Initial | Measured GPU | Measured CPU | Calibrated |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NFR-1 first insert p50, GPU tier (Whisper large-v3-turbo) | 1000 ms | 360 ms (Parakeet 365 ms) | - | 1000 ms |
| NFR-2 first insert p50, CPU tier (Parakeet) | 2500 ms | - | 386 ms (Whisper large-v3-turbo 7046 ms) | 1500 ms |
| NFR-4 transcription realtime factor, GPU tier (Whisper) | 10x | 89x (Parakeet 143x) | - | 20x |
| NFR-5 transcription realtime factor, CPU tier (Parakeet) | 2x | - | 21.6x (Whisper large-v3-turbo 2.0x) | 5x |
| NFR-6 daemon idle resident set | 60 MiB | 41.1 MiB | 42.3 MiB | 60 MiB |
| NFR-8 socket ready p50 | 300 ms | 181 ms | 181 ms | 300 ms |
| NFR-8 app first frame | 300 ms | 395 ms | 349 ms | 300 ms |

The CPU tier is stated for Parakeet. Whisper large-v3-turbo on sixteen Zen 4 cores takes seven seconds to answer an eleven-second dictation and transcribes at about twice realtime on a quiet machine; no headroom makes that a first-insert promise, and a laptop of the CPU tier sits well above it. Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 answers the same dictation in about 400 ms on the CPU and transcribes at over twenty times realtime, so the CPU tier's targets are the figures a Parakeet user gets with the headroom a slower machine needs: NFR-2 moves from 2.5 s to 1.5 s, and NFR-5 from twice to five times realtime. The benchmark's headline for the CPU tier is therefore the Parakeet row (`nfr::headline_provider`), the Whisper row is recorded beneath it with its own verdict, and the README lists both. The consequence for the product is that a CPU-tier machine should run Parakeet by default; until a tier-aware default lands, [docs/engines.md](../engines.md) tells a CPU user to set `[speech] provider = "parakeet"`, and the dictation pack's NFR line on a CPU machine still running Whisper reports the miss by name.

The GPU tier keeps NFR-1 at one second: Whisper answers in 360 ms with a p95 of 524 ms on the 4090, and the second is the headroom a laptop GPU needs. NFR-4 moves from ten to twenty times realtime, a quarter of what the 4090 does with Whisper, because the claim is for the tier and a mid-range Vulkan device should still clear it. NFR-6 keeps 60 MiB: the daemon idles at 41 to 42 MiB in a debug build after both engines have loaded and unloaded, and a release build sits under that. NFR-8 keeps 300 ms for the socket (181 ms in a debug build) and for the app's first frame, which is the one figure both reports miss: 395 ms on the GPU run, whose load average had climbed to eleven by its end, 349 ms on the CPU run, and 244 to 286 ms on quieter runs during the calibration. The target stays at 300 ms because the quiet runs clear it and the app's start is the thing to trim, not the promise; the reports record the miss and the release notes quote it until it is fixed; the app's first frame comes from the `app_routes` drive, which on a desktop whose accessibility bus is wedged runs under a private session bus with its own `at-spi-bus-launcher` and registry, the way the CI helper does.

The language model's rewrite is recorded and not held to a target: on the GPU the Polish goldens' inputs rewrite in 973 ms at p50 (about 150 tokens per second, model load excluded) and on the CPU in about two seconds, both inside `[llm] timeout_ms`. Diarization throughput stays `not_available` until its spec lands.

Bounds: the throughput factor includes the model load, so a short file understates it (the warm-up row records the load's cost); the first-insert figure excludes the real insertion chain's cost, which the dictation pack measures separately; the CPU report comes from a 16-core desktop, so a laptop of the CPU tier will sit above it, and the calibrated CPU targets carry that headroom; a run taken while the machine builds something else is visible in its load average and in the samples' spread, and is rerun rather than quoted. Diarization throughput, the CPU VM and the soak runs stay with their own specs. Two things the runs turned up are recorded for their own specs: the daemon does not answer `system.capabilities` while a CPU Whisper transcription is in flight, and a Whisper CPU dictation that outlasts the twenty-second completion wait fails the step's remaining runs.
