# 0095. A listed term in Whisper's output earns its place against the take decoded without the prompt

Status: Accepted 2026-11-02. Amends 0081 and 0088. Amended 2026-11-02: an unsure lone name stays when the prompt-free decode sounds like it.

## What this gives you

On Whisper, a vocabulary helps you get the names you say spelt as you listed them, and it no longer writes them into sentences where you never said them. Both engines now hold one bar: a listed term comes out when it was said, and not when it was not.

## Situation

Whisper takes the vocabulary as its decoder prompt, so the model decides whether to write a term, and until now nothing checked the result. On the term-recall benchmark, Whisper large-v3-turbo with the vocabulary on wrote 10 of 15 terms. It also wrote 4 terms into 3 of the 25 distractor sentences. "Omar keeps the spare keys in his desk." came back as "Omarchy, Hyprland.", "Clay and I went to the cinema" as "Clef and I went", and "Omar, he said" as "Omarchy said". The prompt-echo guard of ADR 0081 drops a segment only when it is the whole prompt, so these partial echoes were pasted. Parakeet decides each replacement from the audio and wrote no false term (ADR 0088).

whisper.cpp reports a log probability for every token it emits, and one engine state can decode a take twice, once with the prompt and once without it.

## Decision

- **What is checked.** A dictation request to Whisper carries the prompt's terms in `vocabulary`, each with its `sounds_like` spellings as aliases (boost 0, ignored). The prompt itself still names the terms only. A term span is a run of one to four words of one segment whose letters and digits, lower-cased, spell a term, or the term with `s` after it. Only a term in the prompt can be read back from it, so aliases are never looked for as spans; they serve the sound check below. A take whose output spells no term is returned exactly as decoded, with no second decode.
- **Echo shape.** A span is a suspect, however sure the decoder was, when it sits in a run of two or more term spans that closes its segment and either opens the segment or follows a word ending in `.`, `!` or `?`. "Omarchy, Hyprland." and "Book the room. Omarchy, Hyprland." are echo-shaped. "I bound the key in Hyprland." is not, and neither is "Omarchy." on its own: one name alone is what a user says to paste it, so its confidence decides.
- **Token confidence.** Any other span is a suspect when the mean log probability of the tokens under its letters is under -0.3 nats (`MIN_MEAN_LOGPROB`, a geometric mean probability of 0.74). Tokens that do not spell the segment's text make the span a suspect.
- **Settling a suspect.** When any span is a suspect, the engine decodes the take once more on the same state with no prompt, in the language the first decode used. Words both decodes share, matched by their longest common subsequence, anchor the two. A suspect stays when that decode has its words too, or when the gap between its anchors spells the same letters in both ("Sapien XT" for SapienXT). Otherwise the gap becomes the words that decode heard there, or nothing when it heard nothing there. A segment the check rewrote returns no word timings.
- **Sound check for a lone name.** A lone name is a term span that is a sentence of its own and not part of an echo: it opens the take or follows a word ending in `.`, `!` or `?`, and it ends the take or ends in one itself. A segment break is not a sentence end, because Whisper splits segments at timestamps, so "She shaped the" followed by a segment " Clef." is a name inside a sentence. When a lone name is a suspect and the prompt-free decode's gap spells other letters, the gap's letters are scored against the term and each alias with `dettivo_engine_proto::sound::alike`. The score is the share of the longer consonant key (Parakeet's key, ADR 0088) that the shorter one matches, times the ratio of the two sound counts, so a swapped like sound scores above a dropped one. The name stays when its best score is at least 0.6 (`MIN_SOUND_ALIKE`). "Hyperland" for Hyprland and "Oh Marky" for Omarchy score 1.0 against an alias, and "Pleph" for Clef scores 0.667, so all three keep the name. "Clyde" for Clef scores 0.533 and "Omar" for Omarchy 0.500, so those give way. A term inside a sentence never stays by sound. The sound matching moved from the Parakeet engine to `dettivo-engine-proto`, the one crate both engines may depend on, and Parakeet reads it from there.
- **The level** was chosen as fn-84 chose Parakeet's boost: the best level that keeps zero false insertions with no guard WER rise, with recall at least at its earlier figure. Every level from -0.45 to -0.2 does that, with 10 of 15 terms and no false insertion. -0.3 is the round level nearest the middle of that window ([vocabulary-2026-11-02-thor-whisper-threshold](../reports/benchmarks/vocabulary-2026-11-02-thor-whisper-threshold.md)). One level serves both recall and safety on this set, so there is no new setting.
- The engine logs `vocabulary check candidates=<n> suspects=<n> replaced=<n> check_ms=<ms>` per request and each span's mean log probability at debug level, never a word. In CLI mode `--vocabulary '[{"term":"Clef"}]'` beside `--prompt` does the same.

## Consequences

- On the 47-clip set, padded and guarded as dictation does (ADR 0096), Whisper with the vocabulary on writes 10 of 15 terms and no false insertion, guard WER 0.000 against 0.011 with it off. The kept level has 0.12 nats above the least sure target (target-hyprland-1, -0.177) and 0.16 nats below the surest distractor term (distractor-clay-name, -0.458). Above -0.2, target-hyprland-1 gives way to "Piper Lan". Below -0.45, the Clef distractor stays, and below -0.66 the "Omarchy said" distractor stays too.
- The two echo-shaped terms (-0.96 and -1.18) are caught by shape at any level. A confident echo of two or more terms would be caught the same way.
- 33 clips with no term came back byte for byte as before. 10 of the 13 clips with a term needed no second decode. The three that did took 78 to 85 ms more on an RTX 4090 over Vulkan, against a target of 150 ms, so the median over clips with a candidate is 0 ms.
- A take that is one listed name said on its own keeps it when the decoder was sure, or when it was unsure and the prompt-free decode sounds like it. `dettivo-qa bench vocabulary` measures this on every run in its single-name section: each of the five names and five sound-alikes ("Clay.", "Omar.", "Hyper.", "Sapient.", "Detective.") said alone in two voices, with the five-term vocabulary. 6 of 10 names paste and none of the 10 sound-alikes becomes a term ([vocabulary-2026-11-02-thor-sound-check](../reports/benchmarks/vocabulary-2026-11-02-thor-sound-check.md)). That is up from 5 by confidence alone, against 1 when one name alone counted as an echo and 7 with no check. The 47-clip set has no unsure lone name, so its transcripts are unchanged.
- The sound level was chosen on three renderings of the single-name set: the committed one and two fresh syntheses with the same voices ([vocabulary-2026-11-02-thor-sound-check-levels](../reports/benchmarks/vocabulary-2026-11-02-thor-sound-check-levels.md)). Every level from 0.55 to 1.0 gives the most names with no false insertion: 6, 5 and 6 of 10, with no sound-alike as a term. At 0.533 or below, the committed "Clay." (amy), decoded "Clef," at -0.344 and "Clyde." without the prompt, pastes "Clef". Every name the check kept scored 1.0 against an alias. 0.6 is the midpoint between that sound-alike and the 0.667 of "Pleph" for Clef, a swapped sound the decision names as one to keep. It has 0.067 above the surest sound-alike and 0.4 below every kept name. No level reaches 7 of 10 on the committed set without the "Clay." insertion. The names it cannot reach were heard as other words entirely ("Blah.", "Bye." for Clef), or never written as the term at all (Dettivo).
- A `sounds_like` spelling that is a real word keeps the term when it is said alone and Whisper hesitates. Listing `clay` for Clef means a lone "Clay." that Whisper writes as an unsure "Clef" and hears as "Clay" without the prompt pastes "Clef". That is what the entry declares, and no rendering here produced it.
- A looser confidence level for one name alone, -0.45, was measured on 2026-11-02 and rejected before the sound check existed. It kept 7 of 10 names but also the "Clay." sound-alike, at -0.344, which is surer than three of the real names ([vocabulary-2026-11-02-thor-lone-level-0.45](../reports/benchmarks/vocabulary-2026-11-02-thor-lone-level-0.45.json), an uncommitted build on `ff79297`). The sound check keeps the -0.3 level and recovers Hyprland, one of the two names that level reached only with that insertion. Clef (ryan) stays lost, because its prompt-free decode is "Blah.". Silence, a click, breath, hum and noise primed with the vocabulary came back as filler the output guards drop, never as a term.
- A second decode costs 70 to 85 ms once the GPU driver has compiled its pipelines. The first decode without the prompt after the driver's shader cache is emptied (a driver update, or a profile with a fresh `XDG_CACHE_HOME`) compiles them and takes 0.8 to 1.2 s once. The load's silent warm-up (ADR 0084) cannot compile them ahead, because silence never decodes the tokens speech does.
- When a gap holds a rejected term and other words the two decodes disagree on, the whole gap takes the prompt-free words, including a confident term in it.
- File transcription, re-runs and meetings still send the prompt without the terms and are not checked.
