# 0036. A meeting keeps the user's notes and the model's analysis apart, polishes its segments at finalisation, and deletes by the contract's policy

Status: Accepted 2026-09-05, corrected 2026-09-06 by 0053 (the notes migration is 0007-notes-analysis and the index has seven columns)

## What this gives you

A meeting becomes a record you can trust and search. Your notes are Markdown you write during or after the meeting; they live on the row and as `notes.md` beside the audio, and no model ever rewrites them. The analysis is the model's work: a summary, the decisions and the action items over the finalised transcript, stored as `analysis.json` beside the notes, regenerated on request and never overwriting what you wrote. One search finds a word in the title, the transcript, the notes or the analysis and says which. Every export carries the speakers, the timestamps and, in Markdown and JSON, the notes and the analysis, byte-equal to goldens. Delete does exactly what the contract's `artifact_policy` names, so `transcript_only` never takes your notes or your audio with it.

## Situation

The capture spec (ADR 0027) stored a meeting's texts and segments and served `meetings.delete` with a first reading of the artifact policy in which `transcript_and_audio` removed the row. The live transcription spec (ADR 0031) filled the row with the engine's words and nothing else: no polished text, no summary, no notes, no analysis, and a search index over the title and the texts alone. The masterplan asks for more (FR-G6, FR-G8, FR-G9, FR-Y4, FR-Y5): editable notes separate from the transcript, an analysis through the language model layer the Polish spec built (ADR 0023) and the local engine fills (ADR 0026), full-text search over notes and analysis, the five export formats with the macOS JSON keys, and a delete that removes what the policy names and nothing more.

Three choices were open. Where the analysis runs: in the meeting crate at finalisation, or in the daemon after the row settled. Whether the finalisation polishes the transcript, and where the polished text lives. And how a delete that keeps the row treats the search index, the audio and the notes.

## Decision

**Notes and analysis are two columns and two files, and the model touches one of them.** Migration `0007-notes-analysis` adds `notes_markdown`, `notes_source` (`user`, `live`), `notes_updated_at`, `analysis` (the JSON), `analysis_text` (the flattened text the index reads), `analysis_status` (`none`, `running`, `ready`, `failed`), `analysis_error`, `analysis_model` and `analysis_at`. `meetings.notes.set` writes the row and `notes.md` (atomically, through a sibling temporary file) and is allowed while the meeting records; a body over 1 MiB is `INVALID_PARAMS` naming the limit. The analysis job writes `analysis` and `analysis.json`; it reads the notes never and writes them never.

**The analysis is a daemon job over the provider layer, started by the archive hook after the row settled.** `dettivo-language::analysis` carries the prompt contract (one JSON object with `summary`, `decisions`, `action_items` with an optional `owner` and `due`), the strict parser (thinking blocks and code fences stripped, the macOS keys and their camel-case spellings accepted, an empty summary degenerate), the chunking on segment lines at `[meetings.analysis] chunk_chars` with one merge call over the parts (map, then reduce), and one repair pass after a degenerate answer before the run fails naming both reasons. It runs through the same `LlmProvider` interface as Enhanced: `[meetings.analysis] provider` (empty follows `[llm] provider`), the local engine bound to `[llm] analysis_model` (the Enhanced engine itself while the names agree, so one process serves both), Ollama, an endpoint behind the trust gate, or `DETTIVO_MOCK_LLM`. The daemon's `Analysis` service owns the job: `meetings.analyze` starts it (`force` regenerates; `CONFLICT` `analysisRunning`, `meetingNotCompleted`, `endpointNotTrusted`; with no provider the row is `failed` with `provider_unavailable` and the answer carries the notice), the `meeting.state` topic reports `analysis_status`, and a failed run keeps the previous analysis. The meeting crate learns nothing about models: `dettivo_meeting::Archive` gains two hooks, `completed` (before the completed row is stored) and `settled` (after the finalisation slot is freed), and the daemon's `MeetingArchive` implements them; the import path calls the same two.

**The finalisation polishes every segment and the transcript is the polished join.** In `completed`, the daemon runs the deterministic Polish pass (`dettivo-language::polish`, the global `[polish]` transforms, no app rules, no model) over each segment into `polished_text`; `final_text` becomes the polished join and `raw_text` keeps the engine's words. Exports, the analysis and `meetings.get.transcript` read the polished text; `raw = true` on an export reads the engine's words.

**Search is one FTS5 index over seven columns, and a hit names its column.** The index is rebuilt over `raw_text`, `final_text`, `title`, `summary`, `speaker_names` (the column migration `0006-speakers` added, ADR 0035), `notes_markdown` and `analysis_text`; the update trigger keeps it in step, so a notes or analysis write refreshes it in the same transaction. `meetings.search` and `transcripts.search` take the snippet from the first column that matched in the order title, transcript, notes, analysis and name it in `matched_field`.

**Delete removes what the policy names.** `transcript_only` clears the texts, the segments (and with them the speaker labels), the summary, the analysis, `analysis.json` and the search entry; the row, the notes, the audio and the directory stay. `transcript_and_audio` does the same and removes every take and the take sidecars (`takes.json`, `system-takes.json`), forgetting `audio_dir`, `system_audio` and `microphone_takes`; the row's other facts, the notes, the journal and `notes.md` stay. `all` removes the row and the directory. The active meeting is `CONFLICT` `sessionActive`; a running analysis is cancelled first and its result dropped. `[meetings] delete_artifact_policy` (default `all`) is what `dettivo meetings delete` and the app use when none is named. This supersedes the reading in ADR 0027 under which `transcript_and_audio` removed the row.

**Exports render from the row against goldens.** `txt` and `md` label every segment with its speaker or its side and mark the gaps; `srt` and `vtt` prefix each cue the same way; `md` carries a Notes section under the precedence rule (the live editor's `notes_override`, then the saved notes) above the Analysis section (summary, decisions, action items with owner and due) and the transcript; `json` carries the macOS keys (`id`, `title`, `started_at`, `ended_at`, `duration_ms`, `status`, `language`, `stt_provider_id`, `stt_model_id`, `transcript`, `segments`, `analysis`) plus `notes` and `speakers` when present. The seeded roadmap meeting renders byte-equal to `crates/dettivo-storage/tests/goldens/meeting.{txt,md,srt,vtt,json}`.

**The disclosure gets its last pieces.** `dettivo meetings disclosure --copy` puts the macOS text on the clipboard through `insert.perform` in clipboard-only mode (the QA mock sink under `DETTIVO_MOCK_INSERT`), and `DETTIVO_E2E_DISCLOSURE=acknowledged|pending` seeds or clears the acknowledgement in `state.toml` at start so a drive shows the dialog on a fresh profile or skips it.

## Consequences

- The row and the files can disagree only in one direction: the row is what search and export read, the file is the copy a person opens. A file edited by hand is not read back; the next `meetings.notes.set` rewrites it.
- The analysis prompt is this port's own wording of the macOS contract (the macOS source text was not available in this checkout); its output contract and the parser's acceptance of both key spellings are what the goldens under `crates/dettivo-language/tests/goldens/meeting_analysis.json` pin.
- A second `dettivo-engine-llm` process runs only when `[llm] analysis_model` names a model other than `[llm] model`; the daemon tests never do.
- The polished text doubles the stored segment text; a thirty-minute meeting adds a few kilobytes.
- The diarization spec owns `speaker` and the speaker names; every renderer here reads whatever label the row carries and falls back to the side, and the `json` export gains `diarization` there.
- `speaker_count` on `meetings.list` and `speakers` in the exports count distinct labels on the segments, so an unnamed meeting reports zero rather than two sides.
