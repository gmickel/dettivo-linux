## Done summary
Whisper checks every listed term in a prompted dictation transcript (ADR 0095). Two kinds of term are suspects: an echo-shaped run of two or more terms, and a term whose mean token log probability is under -0.3. Each suspect is settled against the same take decoded without the prompt. The output guards moved to dettivo-transcribe, and `dettivo-qa bench vocabulary` passes Whisper the terms and guards each clip as dictation does (ADR 0096). It now also measures Whisper's lone names on every run. After QA, a lone listed name the decoder was sure of pastes, and raw spoken punctuation keeps nouns and ends a sentence once. Parakeet no longer writes `C++++.` for a heard `C++.`. Round 4 adds a sound check: an unsure lone name stays when the prompt-free decode sounds like it.

Round 4 (Gordon's decision 2026-11-02, "Add a sound check"): an unsure lone listed name now stays when the prompt-free decode sounds like the term or one of its `sounds_like` spellings, and gives way otherwise. First, origin/goal/fn-88-better-dictation-shipped-as-one-release was merged in as 4bedb64, which brought in fn-81 1b8c59f. - Shared matching: Parakeet's bias/sound.rs moved to `dettivo_engine_proto::sound`, the one crate both engines may depend on under tools/xtask/src/edges.rs. It gained `sound::alike`, a 0-1 score: the share of the longer consonant key matched, times the ratio of the sound counts. Parakeet reads it from there unchanged (2969282).
- The check (crates/dettivo-engine-whisper/src/check.rs): a lone name is a term span that is a sentence of its own and not part of an echo. A segment break does not count as a sentence end. Such a name, when it is a confidence suspect, stays when its best `alike` against the term and aliases is at least `MIN_SOUND_ALIKE = 0.6`. Whisper requests now carry each prompt term's aliases (dettivo-session bias.rs `checked_terms`, and the bench's `prompt_args`). The prompt itself still names the terms only.
- Level, from measurement (docs/reports/benchmarks/vocabulary-2026-11-02-thor-sound-check-levels.md): the set is the committed single-name set plus two fresh renderings with the same Piper voices and new VITS noise, rendered and benched but not committed. Every level from 0.55 to 1.0 is safe and gives the same recall: names 6/10, 5/10 and 6/10, with 0/10 sound-alikes as a term on each. At 0.533 or below, the committed lone "Clay." (amy) pastes "Clef" (prompted "Clef," at -0.344, prompt-free "Clyde.", which scores 0.533). Every name the check kept scored 1.0 against an alias: Hyperland in renderings 1 and 2, "Oh Marky" in rendering 3. 0.6 is the midpoint between Clyde (0.533) and the decision's own keep example, Pleph for Clef (0.667). Its margins are 0.067 above the surest sound-alike and 0.4 below every kept name. 7/10 is not reached safely on any rendering: the names left are Clef (ryan), heard as "Blah."/"Bye." (0.375 and 0.0), and Dettivo, which the prompted decode never writes. So this ships the best safe level and reports the shortfall, as the decision allows.
